01 — ABOUT ME

My Professional Direction

My name is Jacob Bailey, and I am a Hospitality & Resort Management student at the University of Memphis with a passion for hospitality, entertainment, events, guest experience, and the people who make memorable experiences possible.

But this portfolio is about more than my degree. It is the story of how different experiences have shaped the way I see hospitality.

Music taught me rhythm, timing, teamwork, and how a group of people can create something greater than any one person could create alone.

Martial arts taught me discipline, patience, humility, perseverance, leadership, and the importance of continuing to learn.

American Sign Language taught me that communication is about much more than speaking. It taught me that learning how other people communicate can create opportunities to connect, help, and include people who might otherwise feel overlooked.

Hospitality taught me that service is not simply completing a task. It is about making someone feel welcomed, valued, safe, and remembered.

Entertainment taught me the power of emotion and storytelling.

My childhood experiences at Dollywood with my grandparents became meaningful memories that influenced the way I viewed entertainment, family experiences, and guest service. I even had dreams about Dollywood, which shows how deeply the park captured my imagination.

05 — ASL & INCLUSION

Communication With a Purpose 🤟

At Bowie Reading and Learning Center, I had the opportunity to learn from people who prepared me not only academically, but also for life. It was a great school filled with great people, and the lessons I learned there continued beyond the classroom.

At one point, I was asked if I wanted to take another language. I chose American Sign Language.

I did not choose ASL simply because I wanted another class on my schedule. I wanted to learn something for a purpose. I wanted to learn a way to communicate that could help me connect with people who communicate differently from me.

I wanted to be able to help people. And I wanted to learn something that could potentially allow me to inspire someone else.

Learning ASL helped me understand that effective communication begins with a willingness to learn how another person communicates rather than expecting everyone else to communicate exactly the way I do.

A guest should not have to feel like an inconvenience because they communicate differently. A guest should feel welcomed, respected, and that someone is willing to make an effort.

What ASL Taught Me

  • 🤟 CommunicationCommunication is bigger than spoken words.
  • ❤️ EmpathyUnderstanding another person's perspective matters.
  • 👂 ListeningGood communication begins with paying attention.
  • 🌎 InclusionHospitality should make room for people rather than asking people to fit a single mold.
  • 🤝 ConnectionLearning another person's language can be an act of respect.
  • 💡 PurposeLearning is most meaningful when you can use it to help someone else.

07 — LEADERSHIP

Lead From the Ground Up

I do not define leadership by a job title.

Leadership is what happens when people trust you to contribute, take responsibility, help others, and make the situation better.

My leadership experiences have come through martial arts instruction, marching band, hospitality organizations, Hospitality Nexus, university involvement, professional experiences, and team environments.

My leadership philosophy

I want to know what it feels like to work the front line before I ask someone else to do it.

I want to understand the guest before I make a strategic decision about the guest.

I want to understand the employee before I lead the employee.

And I want to understand the person before I make assumptions about what they need.

🥋 Martial Arts — Discipline Beyond the Dojo

Shotokan Karate and Taekwondo, first-degree black belt, and teaching experience taught me discipline, patience, respect, perseverance, focus, humility, and leadership.

Teaching others taught me to explain clearly, adjust my approach, encourage students, and recognize that everyone learns differently.

The leadership lesson: Earning the right to lead comes from learning how to serve and support others first.

11 — THE STORY

The Story Is Still Being Written

This portfolio is intentionally unfinished.

There are pages here that I have not written yet. There are internships I have not completed. There are jobs I have not held. There are people I have not met. There are projects I have not created. There are places I have not worked. There are lessons I have not learned.

And that is the point.

Every new experience will add another chapter.

🌎 The Legacy I Want to Build

Eventually, I want this portfolio to become much more than a student portfolio. I want to look back and see where I started, who helped me, what I learned, what mistakes I made, what I built, who I worked with, how my thinking changed, and how my career developed.

A career does not have to be perfectly planned from the beginning. You can start with one opportunity, then another. You can learn from people. You can change direction. You can discover something you did not know you loved. You can take lessons from completely different parts of your life and bring them together.

You can choose to learn something because it might help someone else. You can discover that communication, service, entertainment, discipline, music, education, and hospitality are not separate ideas. They can become one story.

⭐ My Promise to My Future Self

I want to build a career that I can be proud of. Not because of how impressive the title sounds. Not because of how many companies I can put on a résumé. Not because of how much money I eventually make.

But because of the people I helped. The experiences I created. The teams I supported. The guests I served. The people I learned from. The memories I helped create. And the people I eventually get the opportunity to teach.

I want my career to tell a story.

A story about hospitality. A story about entertainment. A story about learning. A story about service. A story about people. A story about the lessons I received and the people who gave them to me.

I want to remember that I did not get here alone. And if I am fortunate enough to become a leader someday, I want to make sure the people who come after me know that they do not have to build their careers alone either.

I want to help them. I want to teach them. I want to encourage them. I want to give someone else an opportunity.

Because the people who shaped me did not simply give me knowledge. They gave me pieces of themselves.

I want those lessons to live through me.
I want my career to tell a story.
And this is only the beginning.
